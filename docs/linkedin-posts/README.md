## Your version of the pattern

The difference: hrarfin writes mostly career advice. You write **real engineering stories from shipped work**. That is rarer, and it's what hiring managers, clients and engineers all respect.

### Every post follows this shape

1. **Hook (1 line):** a concrete moment, a number, or a claim people will argue with.
2. **Context (1–2 short paragraphs):** what the system was and what was at stake.
3. **The problem or wrong assumption (1 paragraph).**
4. **The decision (1–2 paragraphs):** what you changed and why. One technical detail an engineer can learn from.
5. **Result (1 line):** honest, no exaggeration.
6. **Takeaway (1 line):** quotable, true beyond this project.

### Rules

- **No emojis, no bold Unicode, no "Here's the thing".**
- **No hashtags** in the post. If you want them, add at most 3 at the end (#React #WebRTC #SaaS).
- **Link only in the first comment.** Post the comment within a minute of publishing.
- **Never cross-post the same text to several groups.** Your current feed shows the same post 4–5 times. Post once on your profile.
- **Reply to every comment in the first 2 hours.** This matters as much as the post.
- **Confidentiality:** MyMedicalHub posts only use facts already public on your LinkedIn or blog (99.9%, 70%, 60 FPS, 40+ useEffects). No client names beyond product names already on your profile, no server specs, no internal URLs.
- **Never claim more than the case study says.** Where a case study says "pre-release" or "not verified in production", the post says so too.
